## Session 1 — The Climb

*The day she saw the whole lamp, and kept it.*

On the Widow's Stair the merchant [[Master Orrin Gilt]] named the turn before the guide did, and told Ilyra he had studied the maps. She noted it. At the wayside shrine she shaped a fireball around her friends and burned three [[Ice Mephit|ice mephits]] out of the air.

Then she held the rubbing she had carried from the valley against the shrine's carving. The same lamp, line for line, and here the flame was whole: nothing chiselled away. In the offering niche under the snow [[Nim Underbough|Nim]] found a [[Silver Votive Lamp|small silver lamp]] with the same design, and Ilyra took it.

At [[Saint Aubren's Rest]] [[Durgan Flintmere]] was waiting, and promised her the tower; at supper he drew the hall's carvings on the tablecloth for her. Partway through the meal she set the silver lamp on the table and asked [[Abbot Severin]] what it was. He turned it in his fingers for a long time: an old ornament of the house, set aside long ago. Might he keep it for the chapel? She said she would rather keep it. He gave it back, smiling.

In the night Nim woke her. Gilt's chest held mail, a longsword and a white surcoat with the whole lamp in gold thread on the breast.

## Session 2 — The Office for Brother Matthias

*Every lamp cut away but one, a hymn with one word changed, and the steam beneath the house.*

[[Durgan Flintmere|Durgan]] kept his promise. In the bell tower he showed her the old lamp carvings, every one cut back to the bare stone except the one over the abbot's door, and her rubbing matched them line for line. "Someone with a chisel and no love for it." He showed her a stair down, blocked with newer stone, and told her to leave it; she did. From the top she saw steam rising from a vent in the glacier, straight below the chapter house.

In the chapel [[Brother Ansel]] was rehearsing, and she knew the hymn at once: the old evening hymn of [[The Lantern at the Pass]], with "breath" where the word should be "light". The abbot had chosen it. Ansel told her, unasked, that the abbot never looked at him the way he looked at the merchant.

At the office she listened for the hymn and watched Gilt and saw nothing in him. She did hear [[Marda Vell]] sing "light" out loud where every brother sang "breath". She drank her cup that night. When she came out into the corridor, the merchant was gone, and the man in his place wore the whole lamp on his breast.

## Session 3 — Peace, Child

*A spell the abbot smiled at, a web across the stair, and a lamp she would not hand over.*

Her *Hold Person* slid off the abbot; he smiled at her. Her rays mostly missed. Then the rest of the order came up the inner stair and she threw a web across the head of it and caught three of the four brothers, and that was the end of the reinforcements.

She read the register with [[Oswin]], who knew every hand in it, and found the Long Night page. She looked for "Nell Underhill" and did not tell Nim she had. When Lucan asked her for the [[Silver Votive Lamp|silver lamp]] she said no, it was hers to carry, and lit it herself while he said the prayer. It burned: the whole lamp. Under the glacier she asked the dragon one question, what the saint had called it. "Guest." "Then I know enough." She slipped on the ice going down, and it was her slip that woke it.

## Session 4 — Long Night Early

*The lamp, the tags, and a list of names until noon.*

She held [[Silver Votive Lamp|the lamp]] up when the breath came and turned her face into the light. Then she stepped up the slope through nothing to stand beside Kael and put fire on it, and it shrugged half of it off. When it carried Lucan under the ice she put three rays of fire down through it. It held on.

Afterwards she read the tags at the collars of the dead in the walls. Every one had a little lamp scratched on it: the register's lamp. She took [[Cloak of Protection|a grey cloak]] from a pilgrim's pack.

At dawn [[Brother Hollis]] wanted to read every name in the register aloud. She sat with him and wrote them down for the families below. He read until noon.
