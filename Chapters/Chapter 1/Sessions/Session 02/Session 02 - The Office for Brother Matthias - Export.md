# Session 02 - The Office for Brother Matthias - Export

# A Funeral for Nobody

Date: Nov 18th, 2026

## Summary
The snow had stopped by the time Kael came back through the gate. Brother Hollis shut it behind him and sent him in for soup, and the party sat down to decide what to do with a day they could not leave.

They split up. Ilyra went with Durgan to see the bell tower he had promised her. Brannoc sat down by the fire with his old sergeant. Kael went to Brother Hollis to ask when exactly Tam had left, and Nim said she was going for a walk.

Hollis let Kael read the guest register at his desk. Tam's name was there, written in fresh ink, with a little lamp drawn beside it. When Kael asked what the lamp meant, Hollis said, "Guests we pray for, child. Every one." Kael closed the book and went to find the others.

Up the tower, Durgan showed Ilyra the old lamp carvings. Every one of them had been chiselled away to the bare stone, neatly, except the one over the abbot's door. Her rubbing matched them line for line. "Someone with a chisel and no love for it," Durgan said. "Not a mason." He pointed out an old stair down from the tower that had been blocked with newer stone, and told her to leave it, because opening it would be loud work. From the top of the tower Ilyra saw steam rising from a vent in the glacier, straight below the chapter house.

By the fire, Aldous Crane gave Brannoc his report. On the first night something heavy had been dragged past his door, going down. And all night, every hour, there had been footsteps from the merchant's room. "A fat man," said Crane, "who walks like a thin one." Brannoc asked him to keep listening.

Nim found Master Gilt in the cloister and walked into him. She came away with his purse, and with the feeling that the man inside the big coat was as thin as a rake. The purse held fourteen gold pieces and a little pewter badge of a lamp, the whole lamp, flame and all.

At lunch they compared notes and argued. Brannoc wanted to go straight to the abbot. Ilyra wanted proof first. Nim thought they should leave as soon as the snow allowed. Kael wanted to see what was behind the frosted door. Abbot Severen stopped by their table on his way past and asked Master Gilt to fetch the lamp oil: "You'll know where." Gilt went, and came back with it.

In the afternoon Kael and Brannoc went down the inner stair. Brother Teodor was sitting on the bottom step, carving a candle, and he stood up when they came. Brannoc tried to talk his way past him. Teodor neither moved nor answered. "We'll come back," said Kael. Ilyra went to the chapel, where Brother Ansel was rehearsing the evening's office and Pip was holding his music. She knew the hymn: it was the old evening hymn of the Lantern at the Pass, with "breath" sung where the word should be "light". Ansel said the abbot had chosen it for tonight. Then he said, without being asked, "The abbot never looked at me the way he looks at that merchant." Nim slept all afternoon.

At dusk the whole house went to the chapel for Brother Matthias. Brannoc and Ilyra went with Gilt. The abbot hummed the hymn as he passed behind Gilt's place. During the singing, old Marda Vell sang "light" out loud where every brother sang "breath", and Ilyra noticed. At the end the abbot announced a vigil for Long Night, the night after next, "to honour our guest the merchant". Gilt thanked him.

Kael and Nim were not at the office. While the house sang, they went down to the frosted door, which had been left unguarded. Nim's pick snapped off in the lock. She tried again with her spare and was still trying when the singing stopped, and they ran.

At supper Gilt announced loudly that somebody in this house was a thief and had taken his purse. Nim ate her soup.

That night Brother Hollis brought warm cups round the guest wing "against the cold". Kael saw him pour Gilt's from a small jug of its own, and knocked the cup out of Gilt's hand as if by accident. Hollis said, "Never mind, child," and did not bring him another. Ilyra drank hers. Nim put hers in her pocket. Brannoc kept watch, and did not see Brother Teodor go by.

After midnight Gilt shouted from his room: "Not tonight, Teodor." Everyone came out. Teodor was standing in Gilt's doorway with a rope and Hollis's lamp. He stepped back without a word. Gilt pulled off his hat, and the fat merchant was gone. In his place stood a thin, older man in a coat of oiled mail and a white surcoat with the whole lamp worked in gold on the breast. Pilgrims stood in every doorway along the corridor. The abbot came down it with his lamp, with Hollis and Ansel behind him.

"I am Lucan," the man said. "You taught me the hymn, Father."

Then, in front of the whole guest wing, he told them what the house did. The cups at supper. The guests carried "down to rest" in the night. The bodies given to the dragon under the glacier, in return for the cure in the ice. The little lamps in the register were the abbot's list of who would go next, "and you wrote me first." The Lantern they had given up and cut off their walls. Kael asked where Tam was. Lucan said, "Below. With the others." Nim gave him back his purse. "I think you dropped this."

The abbot did not deny a word of it. "Peace, child," he said. "Someone must keep the house alive." The brothers closed in around him, and the pilgrims in the doorways looked down at their cups.

## NPCs
### Master Orrin Gilt / Lucan
The fussy merchant the party escorted up the pass turned out to be a thin, older man named Lucan in a magical disguise. He was once the abbot's pupil. He accused the house of theft at supper when his purse went missing, and when Teodor came for him in the night he took off his hat and accused the abbot in front of the house.
