# Session 01 - The Climb - Export

# The Chest on the Ice

Date: Nov 17th, 2026

## Summary
The mule went first. Mid-morning on the pass above Harrowford, on the first shelf of grey ice, its hooves went out from under it and it slid toward the drop, and the rope that tied it to Master Orrin Gilt's locked chest snapped taut. The chest swung out over the fall, turning slowly on its line, and Gilt seized Brannoc's arm and shrieked, "Mind the chest! The mule can look after itself!" Kael ignored him. He got a hand on the mule's halter and dragged the animal back onto the path by main force, and while he did it the chest's rope frayed through and the chest dropped twenty feet onto a ledge below. Gilt was beside himself.

Nim went down for it. She picked her way across the ice on her toes, tied a fresh line around the chest, and in shifting it heard something inside settle with a sound like metal on metal. When she climbed back up and Brannoc and Kael hauled the chest after her, it came up heavy as a grown man. Asked what was in it, Gilt said, "Samples. Fine wool. Very fine." Nim ran her thumb over the lock on the way past and said nothing.

By noon the cloud was building over the col, and Kael made the call to take the Widow's Stair rather than the long switchbacks. Before he could point out the way, Gilt said, "The left fork above the cairn, yes?" When Ilyra asked how a cloth merchant knew the Widow's Stair, Gilt said he had studied the maps, and that he was very thorough. The stair was brutal. Nim slipped twice and reached the top grey-faced and shaking, and the others had to wait while she got her breath back.

At the wayside shrine, a squat stone hood over an old carved lamp, the mule balked and would not go on. Then one of the snowdrifts sat up and laughed. Four ice mephits came out of the drifts, spiteful little things of frost and malice, breathing cold over Brannoc and Kael and clawing at anything warm. Ilyra shaped a fireball around her friends and dropped it into the middle of them, and three of the creatures burst apart into clouds of freezing shards, which caught Brannoc full in the face twice. The fourth fled shrieking up the mountainside. When it was over Gilt knelt beside Brannoc, who was bleeding from a dozen frost cuts, and murmured something under his breath with a hand on his shoulder. The cuts closed. "Luck," said Gilt. Brannoc said he had seen army chaplains do that, and it was not luck. Gilt began to talk about the weather.

Ilyra unfolded the rubbing she had carried from the valley and held it against the shrine's carving. It was the same lamp, line for line, except that here nothing had been chiselled away: the flame was whole. Nim, digging around the base of the shrine, found an offering niche under the snow. Inside were two potions of healing, a frost-blue bottle that Ilyra named a potion of resistance against the cold, thirty-eight coins of an old minting, and a small silver votive lamp worked with the same whole design. Ilyra took the lamp. Kael and Brannoc took a healing potion each, and Nim took the blue one.

At dusk, with the first flakes coming down, the cloud parted and Kael saw the lamps of Saint Aubren's Rest above them: a ladder of lit windows cut into the mountain, and the sound of a bell. It looked like the safest place in the world.

Brother Hollis met them at the gate with a lamp, soup and blankets, and would not hear of anyone signing the guest register before they were warm, and then made sure every one of them did. Gilt signed with a flourish. Brannoc signed with his old company rank. Nim signed herself Nell Underhill. The guest wing was warm and full of pilgrims, and every one of the four found someone they knew. Tam Rowe, Kael's brother-in-law, threw his arms around him and asked about Hesta before he had let go; Kael gave him a tin of her salve. A blind old soldier by the fire turned his head at the sound of Brannoc's voice and said, "Hale. You still walk like you're carrying the regiment's kettle." It was Aldous Crane, his old sergeant. A dwarf mason, Durgan Flintmeer, was delighted that Ilyra had come and told her to wait until she saw the tower. And a woman with a tray of relics cried, "Nimmy!" across the room and asked at the top of her voice how the debts were going. Nim pressed ten of the old coins into her cousin Rosie's hand to make her stop. Rosie bit one, said "Old coin!" just as loudly, and pocketed the lot.

Supper was in the refectory, monks and pilgrims at one long table. Abbot Severen rose to say grace, and at "by Thy breath" an old woman knitting at the end of the bench, Marda Vell, frowned and said "light" under her breath. Brother Ansel led the singing in a fine voice. Sister Wren, tired and smelling of camphor, asked after any wounds from the shrine and offered Brannoc a salve she said was made with ice from the mountain; Brannoc thanked her and said he was mended. Durgan sketched the hall's carvings on the tablecloth for Ilyra. Pip Callow, a boy of about fifteen, sat beside Ansel and copied the way the monks held their bowls. Crane asked Brannoc to describe the faces around the table, and Brannoc did, one by one, the way he would have reported a column. Partway through the meal Ilyra set the silver lamp from the shrine on the table and asked the abbot what it was. The table went quiet. Severen picked it up and turned it in his fingers for a long moment, and said it was an old ornament of the house, a thing the brothers had set aside long ago, and asked whether he might keep it for the chapel. Ilyra thanked him and said she would rather keep it. He smiled and gave it back. Later Brother Hollis brought Tam a cup to help him sleep, and Tam, worn out with the climb and with talking about Hesta, drank it and went to bed early.

Brannoc kept a watch in the corridor that night out of old habit. Some hours after midnight he heard something heavy being dragged, and followed the sound to an inner stair, where an enormous monk with frost in his beard, Brother Teodor, was carrying a sack over his shoulder. Teodor said a brother had died, and was going down to rest. He would not let Brannoc follow him past the frosted door at the bottom of the stair. He was gentle about it, and he did not move.

Nim had gone out too. Gilt's chest had been too heavy to carry upstairs and had been left in a storeroom off the guest wing, and Nim had already had a good look at the lock. Inside, under a layer of folded wool, was a coat of oiled mail, a longsword, and a white surcoat with a lamp worked in gold thread on the breast: the whole lamp, flame and all, the same as the shrine. She heard footsteps and a candle coming and hid behind the flour sacks. It was Gilt, in his nightshirt. He put his hand on the chest's lock, stood there a moment with his lips moving, and went back up. Nim locked the chest again, went upstairs, and woke Ilyra to tell her.

The snow came in the night. In the morning the world outside the windows had gone soft and white and silent, and Tam's place at the long breakfast table was empty. "Your friend left before dawn, poor soul," said Brother Hollis. "Grief makes men foolish." Brannoc put his spoon down and said, loudly enough for the whole table, that Brother Teodor had carried something heavy down the inner stair in the night, and asked which brother had died. Nobody spoke. Then the abbot smiled and said it was Brother Matthias, after a long illness, and that they would say the office for him that evening, and that the guests would be very welcome.

Kael went out into the snow. There were no tracks anywhere: not on the path, not at the gate, not on the slope below. Back inside, he found Tam's pack still under his bed, with Hesta's salve in it. Kael took the salve and set off down the pass on foot anyway. A hundred yards below the gate the snow was up to his waist, and he could see at once that nobody could have gone down that way, and nobody would go down it again before spring. When he turned back, Brother Hollis was holding the gate open for him with a lamp.

## NPCs
### Master Orrin Gilt
A fussy, loud cloth merchant who hired the party to escort him up the pass. He cares more for his locked chest than for his mule, knows the Widow's Stair better than a merchant should, and closed Brannoc's wounds with a murmured word he called luck. At night he came down in his nightshirt to pray over the chest's lock.

### Brother Hollis
The monk who keeps the gate and the guest register. Warm and fussing, he insists every guest is fed and signed in. He brought Tam a sleeping cup at supper and told the party at breakfast that Tam had left before dawn.

### Abbot Severen
The abbot of Saint Aubren's Rest, warm and smiling. He said grace with "breath" for "light", asked to keep Ilyra's silver lamp for the chapel, and at breakfast named the dead brother as Brother Matthias.

### Brother Ansel
A monk with a fine voice who led the singing at supper. Pip sits beside him.

### Sister Wren
A tired nun smelling of camphor who tends the sick. She offered Brannoc a salve made with ice from the mountain.

### Brother Teodor
An enormous, silent monk with frost in his beard. Brannoc found him carrying a heavy sack down an inner stair at night; he said a brother had died and would not let Brannoc past the door at the bottom.

### Brother Matthias
A monk the abbot says died in the night after a long illness. His office is to be said this evening.

### Tam Rowe
Kael's brother-in-law, a pilgrim who came up to pray for Kael's sick sister Hesta. Overjoyed to see Kael, he drank a sleeping cup at supper and was gone by morning, his pack and Hesta's salve still under his bed.

### Aldous Crane
A blind old soldier, Brannoc's sergeant from his old company. He knew Brannoc by his voice and had him describe every face at the supper table.

### Durgan Flintmeer
A dwarf stonemason staying at the monastery, who wrote to Ilyra. He promised to show her the tower and sketched the refectory's carvings on the tablecloth.

### Rosie Tallowby
Nim's cousin, selling relics to the pilgrims. She greeted Nim loudly about her debts and took ten old coins to be quiet, then announced them.

### Marda Vell
An old widow who knits by the fire. She corrected the abbot's grace under her breath.

### Pip Callow
A boy of about fifteen who sat beside Brother Ansel and copied the way the monks hold their bowls.

## Locations
### The Pass
The mountain road from Harrowford up to Saint Aubren's Rest, with an ice shelf above a long drop and a choice between the long switchbacks and the steep Widow's Stair. Waist-deep snow closed it on the first night.

### The Wayside Shrine
A squat stone hood over a carved lamp beside the road, where four ice mephits nested in the drifts. Its carving shows the whole lamp, flame and all. An offering niche under the snow held potions, old coins and a silver lamp.

### Saint Aubren's Rest
A monastery cut into the mountain, its windows lit like a ladder at dusk.

### The Guest Wing
Warm rooms full of pilgrims beside the gate, where Brother Hollis keeps the guest register. A storeroom off it holds the luggage too heavy to carry upstairs.

### The Refectory
The long hall where the monks and the pilgrims eat together and the abbot says grace.

### The Inner Stair
A stair going down inside the monastery, ending at a frosted door. Brannoc met Brother Teodor on it at night.

## Items
### Gilt's Chest
A heavy locked chest Gilt guards closely and says holds wool samples. It holds a coat of oiled mail, a longsword and a white surcoat with a golden lamp on the breast.

### Silver Votive Lamp
A small silver lamp from the wayside shrine's offering niche, worked with the whole lamp design. Ilyra keeps it; the abbot asked for it.

### Ilyra's Rubbing
A rubbing of a lamp carving with half of it chiselled away, which Ilyra carried up from the valley. It matches the shrine's carving.

### Hesta's Salve
A tin of salve Kael brought for Tam from his sister Hesta. Kael found it in Tam's pack after Tam disappeared.

### Potion of Resistance
A frost-blue potion from the shrine's niche, which Nim carries.

### Potions of Healing
Two potions from the shrine's niche, carried by Kael and Brannoc.

### The Guest Register
The monastery's book of arrivals, kept by Brother Hollis. Nim signed it as Nell Underhill.
